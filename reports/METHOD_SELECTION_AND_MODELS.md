# Method selection and models actually executed

The principal estimand is an **exact design quantity of observed histories**. The methods were chosen to compute it exactly, describe its sampling variability, bound it under missingness and label error, and test one operational design choice out of year. No predictive model was fitted in the trial-data analysis (see §7). Two models were fitted elsewhere in the project, for different stages: a logistic completeness model for the inverse-probability-weighting sensitivity check (row 7 below), and a logistic regression ranking benchmark for the World Bank second-contact decision problem (§8). Neither is used to compute the primary estimand.

## Derivations and correctness notes

**Method 1.** The probability that a uniform m-subset of n windows contains none of the K positives is the hypergeometric zero-success probability C(n−K, m)/C(n, m). Averaging over reference-positive households gives the expected *share* missed. Expected *counts* are also reported (`expected_missed_households`). Households with K = 0 are outside the denominator by definition.

**Method 3 (sharpness).** M_m is the mean of A(K_i) = C(n−K_i, m) over the included set S = {i : K_i > 0}. Households with k_i > 0 are forced into S. Households with k_i = 0 and r_i > 0 are optional, and households with k_i = r_i = 0 are excluded. For any fixed S the mean is minimised by each member's smallest attainable A (K = k + r), and every value in {k, …, k + r} is attainable by some completion. Given those values, a minimum-mean superset of the forced set contains exactly the optional items whose value is below the optimum, which is a prefix of the ascending order. Evaluating all prefixes is exhaustive, so the result is attained and optimal. The upper bound is symmetric, with K = max(k, 1). Exact `fractions.Fraction` arithmetic is used, so there is no tolerance.

**Method 4 (flip cost).** With a positive→negative flips, b negative→positive flips and u positive fills of missing windows, K = k − a + b + u, with a ≤ k, b ≤ o − k and u ≤ r. The minimum a + b is max(0, k − K, K − k − r), and it is feasible for every K ≤ n because o + r = n. This confirms the earlier proposal and adds the feasibility argument; feasibility does not bind. Optimisation: Dinkelbach's parametric method with each subproblem F(λ) = min Σ R_i (A_i − λ) subject to Σ cost ≤ B solved exactly by a multiple-choice knapsack DP over the integer budget, using integer arithmetic (λ = P/Q, values scaled by Q). Finite termination at F(λ*) = 0 certifies global optimality. All 192 optimisations (96 configurations = 2 countries × 2 scopes × 6 values of m × 4 budgets, each minimised and maximised) terminated in ≤ 4 Dinkelbach iterations with certificate 0 (`label_error_bounds.csv`). The slowest run took 0.17 s (`logs/solver_runtimes.csv`, local only). Zero-positive worlds are excluded by definition; with the budgets used they are never feasible.

**Method 2 (bootstrap).** Resampling units are villages with at least one complete-history household (Malawi 140, Liberia 47); 2,000 draws; seed 20261026; numpy `linear` (Hyndman–Fan type 7) percentiles. No replicate had a zero denominator. The Liberia distribution is lattice-like (34 positives), so endpoints are coarse. BCa was not used: with 47 clusters the acceleration estimate is itself noisy, and it was not needed to reproduce or interpret results. The jackknife SE is reported as a cross-check.

**Method 5 (timing).** Only the four windows observed in both years are used, which keeps the design separate from the six-window estimand. The random-window benchmark in each cohort uses the same denominator. The 2020 selection is re-run on 2,000 village-bootstrap resamples to measure selection stability (winner's-curse diagnostic). The 2021 evaluation is bootstrapped with the frozen slot held fixed.

## 7. Should a machine-learning model be trained? NO-ML justification

1. **Wrong target.** The estimand is a deterministic functional of observed histories under a known sampling design (random retention of windows). Its exact value is computed; a model cannot estimate it more accurately.
2. **A defensible ML question was considered:** prospective prediction of HHS ≥ 2 at the next designated window from information available at the current window (past HHS, season, baseline covariates), with household/village-grouped, chronological splits (train 2020, test 2021). It was **rejected** because:
   - *Decision use is undefined.* No programme rule (e.g. "re-screen households with predicted risk > t") exists against which value could be measured. The scientific question is about schedules, not individual risk scores.
   - *Data are thin where it would matter.* Liberia's 2021 test set has 60 complete households and 22 reference-positive households. Malawi's lean-season signal is a single calendar effect that a seasonal baseline already captures (Jan–Feb positive share 66% in 2020 vs 15% in Jul–Aug).
   - *Leakage risk with little upside.* Panel conditioning, attrition and seasonality would make apparent AUC gains hard to interpret, and a reviewer would rightly ask what a risk score adds to the ascertainment question.
3. **What would change this decision:** a programme partner specifying a targeting rule and cost structure, or data from several years and more clusters, so that a risk-based adaptive schedule could be evaluated prospectively against fixed schedules.

No benchmark charts, AUCs or feature importances are reported, because none were computed.
