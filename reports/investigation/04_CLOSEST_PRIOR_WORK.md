## What this study can claim to add

These claims rest on the evidence above, and the literature search was not exhaustive.

1. **Timing at a fixed contact budget, measured exactly.** The question is how many of the households that report hunger at any observed contact a single contact misses, and how that depends on which month. It is answered with an exact (not simulated) benchmark, in two Malawi panels with different instruments.
2. **Out-of-time evaluation of choosing the month from data, including when it fails** (training data that begin at the pandemic onset and first interview). I found no prior study that tests this.
3. **Exact decision robustness** for a screening-schedule contrast: assumption-free missing-data ranges plus a tipping-point budget of answer errors.
4. **Practical second-contact targeting** using information already collected at the first contact, with a model trained on an earlier year that adds nothing over a simple count.

**It cannot claim:**
- to be the first to show that food insecurity fluctuates;
- to be the first to show that single surveys misclassify households;
- to have discovered that lean-season data collection finds more households (existing guidance already says so);
- generalisability beyond Malawi.
